# 01 — Experience Vision

## Working title

**Código Rojo: Cancún 2035**

## Format

Immersive technological escape room / professional simulation.

## Audience

Prospective students, primarily high-school students, visiting Universidad Anáhuac Cancún through Día OV, Open House, preparatory-school visits, and future HUB activities.

## Capacity

- Minimum playable: 4
- Optimal: 6–8
- Maximum target: 10

The experience must remain playable when turnout is low.

## Session length

Target: 20–25 minutes.

Suggested rhythm:
- 2 min: cold open / briefing
- 12–15 min: investigation and locks
- 3–5 min: final containment decision
- 2–3 min: reveal/debrief and academic connection

## Desired emotional arc

1. **Intrigue** — something is wrong, but nobody explains exactly what.
2. **Agency** — participants realize they can investigate the environment.
3. **Discovery** — separate clues begin to connect.
4. **Pressure** — the central system degrades and time runs down.
5. **Synthesis** — the team reconstructs what happened.
6. **Responsibility** — they must make a consequential final decision.
7. **Payoff** — the system responds, the story is revealed, and their actions are contextualized professionally.

## Core design principle

The room should make participants behave like a small multidisciplinary incident-response team without needing prior technical knowledge.

The educational content is embedded in the mechanics:
- Cybersecurity = evidence, identity, access, propagation, containment.
- AI = anomaly detection, pattern analysis, confidence, interpretation.
- Professional practice = uncertainty, collaboration, prioritization, consequence.

## Physical setup

### Central LED
Acts as the shared situational-awareness display:
- system health,
- city/system map,
- countdown,
- incident severity,
- recent events,
- visual reaction to progress.

It must **never become a quest tracker**.

### Investigation stations
Planned stations:
1. Access & Identity
2. Mail / Social Engineering
3. Network & Infrastructure
4. AI Anomaly Analysis
5. Final Response Console

Stations can be 2–4 computers depending on available hardware. Multiple roles may share a station.

## Replayability

The first pilot may be deterministic. The architecture and content should later allow:
- alternate suspicious users,
- alternate phishing messages,
- different anomaly patterns,
- multiple incident narratives,
- difficulty levels,
- school-specific variants,
- expanded physical props.

Do not build all of that now; preserve the concept without overengineering the pilot.
