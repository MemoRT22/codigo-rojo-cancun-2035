# 06 — Pilot Plan

## Objective

Validate whether the experience is fun, understandable, immersive, and operational with a real small group before expanding scope.

## Pilot success criteria

Participants should:
- begin investigating without being told every action,
- discover at least one clue through their own observation,
- discuss evidence with one another,
- understand the relationship between multiple stations,
- reach the final response console in the target time range,
- be able to explain what happened after the reveal,
- associate the activity with real AI/Cybersecurity work.

## Things to observe

Do not ask only “did you like it?”. Observe:
- Where do they look first?
- Which controls do they assume are interactive?
- Which clue is too obvious?
- Which clue is unfair?
- When do they stop collaborating?
- Do they understand AI confidence correctly?
- Does the central screen add urgency or distract?
- How many facilitator interventions were required?
- Did they feel a genuine reveal at the end?

## Prototype order

1. Lock the story and answer.
2. Build the central LED visual prototype.
3. Build Mail and Access stations first because they establish the causal chain.
4. Build Infrastructure.
5. Build AI analysis.
6. Build final response console and outcome.
7. Connect state only after each station works as an independent experience.
8. Conduct pilot.
9. Modify puzzles based on observation.

## Deliberately out of scope for first pilot

- user accounts,
- cloud persistence,
- analytics dashboards,
- broad automated testing,
- complex facilitator CMS,
- procedural puzzle generation,
- multiple scenarios,
- remote multiplayer,
- production-grade security architecture.
